THE UNIVERSE DECK

By Abhishek Lamba

The Universe Deck is a deck of cards of 54 unique cards, designed to gamify Museum of Art and Photography's "Beneath The Turning Sky" exhibit.

Each card is double-sided, representing the dual nature of reality. One side of the deck contains the fundamental building blocks of the universe-quarks, electrons, photons; all the ingredients that make up our universe.

The other side contains a high-resolution image fragment of one of the paintings in the exhibit. No two fragments are identical, making every card a unique "coordinate" in the game's universe.

When you place all 54 cards down you would form 4 complete artworks. Meanwhile, the other side would contain the fundamental particles of the universe.

Taken in unison, this single deck of 54 cards offers a complete picture of the universe-one side depicting how science unravels it, and the other side showing how human consciousness interprets it.

This deck of cards can be played in 3 different ways, with each game focusing on a different section of the exhibit. If you're alone, you can solo the deck and puzzle all the paintings on your own!

Game 1: Wonder

Intro+Setup:

  • Inspired by the first section of the exhibit: Wonder, in the game, whoever collects all the cards and ignites the Big Bang, wins.
  • Shuffle the deck and distribute them between all players. Players must keep their deck of cards painting side up in front of them.
  • Players have to announce which hand they are drawing with and which hand they are slapping with before the start of the game.
  • At the start of every turn, without looking, players must take the top card from their deck and place it in the play area, quark side facing up.

There are specific cards, or combination of cards, that if played, any player on the table may slap the deck and claim as their own.

The Slap Rules:

  • You can slap when a particle and its matching antiparticle appear one after the other. When this happens, both particles annihilate each other.
  • Or if three cards are played consecutively, of which 2 are up quarks and 1 is a down quark. These can be in any order. 2 up quarks and 1 down quark make a proton.
  • Similarly, if three cards are played consecutively, of which 2 are down quarks and 1 is an up quark. These can be in any order. 2 down quarks and 1 up quark make a neutron.
  • If a God Particle shows up, all players must switch the hand their drawing hand with their slapping hand.

Game End:

  • If you lose all your cards, you're eliminated.
  • If you win all cards in play, you win.

Game 2: Conquest

Intro + Setup:

  • In this game, inspired by the Exploration and Conquest section of the exhibit, players must compete with one another to create the biggest picture possible.
  • Shuffle all cards, and give 4 to each player.
  • Players must hold their cards with their painting side facing towards them.
  • Keep the rest of the cards in the same deck with the quark side facing up.
  • Keep 1 card with the painting side facing up beside the draw deck.

In Your Turn:

  • Draw 1 card from either the draw pile or the discard pile.
  • You can have a maximum of 4 cards at any point of time. If you have more than 4 cards, discard any extra cards into the discard pile, painting side facing up.
  • If you have two cards that are contigious i.e. they belong to the same painting and are adjacent, then you may place them both down in front of you.
  • As you get more cards that are contigious with any two you have put down, you can keep adding them to your painting in your turn.
  • If you have a God Particle, you can put it back anywhere in the draw deck and take a card from there in its stead.

Game End:

  • The game ends when all paintings cards in the draw pile run out. God Particle Cards may remain in the deck.
  • The player who has the most complete picture of 1 painting, wins.
  • If players are tied, among the tied players, the player with the biggest second painting wins. If still tied, move on to the third painting, and so on.

Game 3: Future, Present

Intro+Setup

  • Inspired by the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle and the Future, Present section of the exhibit, in this game, you must cooperatively assemble all four 4 paintings as you attempt to heal the universe.
  • Pull out all the God Particle cards. For Round 1, distribute 2 cards to every player. For Round 2, distribute 4 cards to every player. For the last round, distribute all remaning cards evenly between players.
  • Players must hold the cards with the painting side facing away from them. At no point can players see the painting side of their own paintings.

Observation Phase:

  • Each player may give 1 Clue to a teammate by pointing to cards in their hand and naming 1 Painting Suit (e.g., "These two cards are Vishwarupa").
  • You're only allowed to give a clue about 1 painting.
  • Each teammate can see the subatomic position of their particle but not the painting which it belongs to. You must work together to get the complete picture.

Metta Phase:

  • There are no turns. Any player may place a card quark side-up into its corresponding slot on any painting grid at any time i.e. place all the cards in the right order.
  • There are 6 God Particle cards in play. You can use up any of the God Particle Cards to give players new clues.
  • Gaps are Legal: You do NOT need to wait for Slot 2 to be filled before placing Slot 3. A Mistake occurs if a player places a card in the wrong slot/suit, or plays a card when another player holds a lower-numbered card of that same painting in their hand. Lose a God Particle if you make a mistake.

Game End:

  • The game ends in failure if you lose all 6 God Particles.
  • You have five minutes to complete each round. The game ends in failure if you run out of time for a round.
  • On easy, you have 7 minutes per round, 6 for Medium, and 4 for Hard.
  • You win if you successfully complete all 4 grids.
  • When the game ends, flip all your cards to the painting side to see the complete picture, and how much of it you got right.
